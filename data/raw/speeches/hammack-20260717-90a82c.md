# LinkedIn Post: No Conflict in Our Mandate — hammack — 2026-07-17

## Highlights

Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, in a LinkedIn post, says there is no conflict in the mandate: inflation is too high while the labor market is right around her level of maximum employment. The Cleveland Fed's PCE nowcast puts core inflation at 3.3 percent after this week's data, and she calls persistently high inflation the bigger concern, citing broad-based pressures reported by district contacts.

---

There is no conflict in our mandate.

Inflation is too high. The labor market is right around my level of maximum employment.

Our PCE nowcast shows core inflation at 3.3 percent after incorporating this week's data.

Persistently high inflation is the bigger concern.

When formulating my policy view, I consider the data as well as conversations I've had with business and community leaders in the Fourth Federal Reserve District.

To effectively set monetary policy, I need to think about not just where the economy was, or where it is today, but also consider where things are heading in the next six or 12 months.

That's why I have conversations with business and community leaders on the ground to learn about what the data may not yet be showing.

What I'm hearing from those conversations is that inflation isn't coming from only one source—it's broad based.

Business leaders point to energy costs and supply chain disruptions as factors but also cite pressures from insurance and the AI data center build up.

At the same time, I'm seeing good growth numbers and stable consumer spending.

For the first time in my tenure, I'm hearing from businesses who say they think we need to take action to curb inflation, and from consumers who can't make ends meet about a growing sense of despair.

I go into every FOMC meeting with an open mind and one goal—to deliver the best outcomes for the American people.

(Source: Beth Hammack, LinkedIn post, 2026-07-17. Hammack is a voting FOMC member in 2026.)
