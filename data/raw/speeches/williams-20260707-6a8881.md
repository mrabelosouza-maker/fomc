# Fox Business Interview — williams — 2026-07-07

*Interview of New York Fed President John C. Williams on Fox Business. Q&A transcript.*

**Fox Business:** John Williams, president of New York Federal Reserve, thank you for being here and doing this with us. I wanted to start quickly with the state of the economy. We have gas prices falling. We have consumer spending still strong. Where do you see the economy right now?

**John Williams:** Basically, I see more of the same, if you will. We're seeing steady growth, with GDP growth around 2%. I feel that's around the trend growth rate. We saw that last year. We're seeing that in the first half of this year.

The labor market also, we're seeing stability. The unemployment rate's been in a kind of narrow range around 4% to 4.25%, around 4.25%, and that continued in June.

We're seeing positive job growth, and other indicators of the labor market are showing, again, stability, solid labor market. So that's, I think, the big picture: continued solid growth.

**Fox Business:** I want to put inflation in this. Energy prices have pushed inflation recently, but gas prices are now down about 11% in the last month or so. How fast do you see inflation then falling this year, and is it faster than you thought?

**John Williams:** Well, I think that is part of the good news. We definitely have seen a big decline in oil prices, not only the current oil prices, but future expected oil prices.

So absolutely, I expect to see in the next few months energy prices to come down quite a bit, and that will bring headline inflation down.

So today, headline inflation on the PCE index is around 4%. I expect that to come down each of the next several months as energy prices come down. And also, as those energy prices come down, we'll see that show up also in core inflation, as airfares and some of the other prices also start to come down.

**Fox Business:** So with this quick move then, do you see a rate hike off the table?

**John Williams:** Well, first of all, I think you have to look at the totality of the data: what's happening on the labor market, the economy, and also inflation. And inflation still is quite high.

We're still seeing inflation from the tariffs, although I think we're near the peak effect of that. We are seeing the decline in energy price inflation, which will affect us. But we're also seeing some inflation from the AI investment boom.

So we have to look at all the different factors. Right now, my view is that monetary policy is well positioned. So I think the interest rates, the stance of policy, is well positioned to achieve our maximum employment and price stability goals.

**Fox Business:** That sounds like a pause. Could we see a pause then for the rest of the year, possibly no movement?

**John Williams:** Well, I think it really depends on what happens with the data, again, in terms of the totality of all the data we're looking at, and also the risks and the evolution of the risks to the economic outlook.

I've always been a believer in being data dependent. So right now, I think policy is in a good place. We just have to see how the economy evolves over the coming months.

**Fox Business:** Is the risk of inflation then decreasing for you and the risk of employment or the labor side of it increasing?

**John Williams:** No, I think on the labor market, I feel pretty good about it. I think the risks are pretty balanced on the labor market.

Again, we're seeing steady, solid growth, steady labor market. On inflation, absolutely inflation is still too high. I do feel a little bit more positive about the near-term inflation outlook because of the energy price declines that we're going to see.

**Fox Business:** We have a new Federal Reserve chairman, Kevin Warsh. He's not putting out forward guidance in the Fed statement. Are you going to still put out forward guidance related to where you think the federal funds rate should be?

**John Williams:** Well, in terms of my own speaking, I regularly give my views on the economic data, the outlook, and how I see monetary policy. Not so much forward guidance, but like we just discussed, how I see monetary policy positioned in terms of achieving our goals, and I plan to continue to do that.

**Fox Business:** What do you think about removing forward guidance? Does it leave a void for the markets?

**John Williams:** Well, I think if you think about the FOMC statement in terms of that forward guidance, given the uncertainties that we face in terms of inflation and the economic outlook, trying to give explicit forward guidance about where interest rates could be was no longer appropriate.

The uncertainties are too great. So I think it is appropriate — obviously strong agreement — to remove the forward guidance from the statement.

**Fox Business:** We're seeing $11 trillion in investments that were announced last year. When will that turn into jobs? We know Micron in your district is a big expansion going on. Do you think we're going to see a flood of jobs from that investment, maybe this year, toward the end of this year?

**John Williams:** Well, I definitely expect to see continued strong investment in the AI and the data center space.

What we are seeing is, I think, a movement of labor from other kinds of construction — residential construction and office buildings and things — into some of the technology and data centers.

So I think it's more of a shift of labor demand from one sector to another. But I do expect that to be an important source of growth for the economy over the next couple years.

**Fox Business:** John Williams, we're going to have to leave it there. Appreciate it. Thank you for your time.
